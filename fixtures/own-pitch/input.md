# The original pitch for Furcule, spoken, 2026-09-18

The idea is simple. Let's say that now there are a lot of variables and things that are happening, and then a conclusion has been drawn about what happened. The project idea is to help with the assumptions that are being made, and then what could that lead us to if the assumptions were taken out of the picture.

I was watching a series called The Blacklist. Raymond Reddington was watching a tape of himself telling him how they can find the answer, because there is always an answer. But the issue is that sometimes you may have made an assumption that's false, which made you draw a conclusion which is also false. So taking a step back and trying to understand the wrong assumptions that were made would help a lot.

Let's say I have an idea for a project and I spill the idea out. Maybe in there is an assumption that's made. What the project should do is help build a graph of every premise or assumption and how it's connected to the next one. Rather than making the idea a block of text, it's made as a graph, and then we can see what conclusions have been drawn from what assumption or fact. Then we can use it another way: with an AI agent that helps us take a step back from each one and see, if that assumption weren't made, what other assumption could take its place, and help us get to a better conclusion.

In the episode he had a box of recordings in a safe, and only him and another person, who is dead, could open that safe. We made an assumption that the only way to get to the recordings is to open the safe. But that assumption was wrong. The whole safe had been removed from the wall and replaced with an empty replica. It's hard to draw that conclusion. You always try to go to the logical thing: it's only opened by me and another guy, there's no way the other guy opened it, and I didn't open it. So you can't draw a logical conclusion. But because we took a step back and came up with another assumption, that the safe wasn't opened but removed as a whole, we can go on: maybe the guy who built the safe was able to build another one. The new assumption helps us see things from a different angle. We have a root, we look at the branch we were stuck at, which is the safe, and when we try different assumptions it takes us down a new path.

The same thing can happen with anything. A lawsuit a lawyer is handling: maybe there is an assumption in the case that was wrong, and exploring it from other sides might help. A detective on a murder case. So we should generalize the idea. There is a project called graphify on my machine that does this for codebases; we can take inspiration from how the graph is made, but ours should be about ideas, concepts and logical conclusions. It can also help researchers: maybe along the way they made an assumption and worked on it, and if they took a step back on one of the assumptions and worked another branch they might find better results, like the KAN paper research.

It should be visually good. Ideas start from a root and expand, so the graph is mostly directional. The user can say, if we change this point we get to another conclusion, and start a new branch, and see the difference between the current one and the new one. It should be easy to go through the graph. And there should be some discrete math built in, proof and contradiction: since this is a conclusion we came to, if we contradict it, it should be false, so we try to contradict it to come to a better conclusion.
