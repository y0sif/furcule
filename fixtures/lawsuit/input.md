# Harlan v. Northline Decks

Summary prepared for counsel. The Harlans hired Northline Decks to build a raised timber deck in March 2024. The contract carries a five-year structural warranty. On 2 August 2025 the deck collapsed during a family gathering; two guests were injured. The Harlans sue Northline for breach of warranty and negligence.

Northline's site inspector, Mr Teague, testified that a building permit was filed before construction and that the county inspected the footings. The county records office has no permit on file for the address and no inspection record for 2024.

The Harlans' own contractor, hired in May 2025 to install a hot tub on the deck, states that he reinforced the joists under the tub area and did not alter the rest of the structure. The hot tub, filled, weighs roughly 2,200 kilograms. The original plans show the deck rated for typical residential occupancy.

The engineering report, commissioned by the Harlans, finds that the ledger board pulled away from the house because the lag bolts were too short for the ledger thickness. It does not analyse load.

Plaintiff's counsel intends to argue that the deck failed within the warranty period from a construction defect, so Northline is liable, and that the missing permit shows Northline cut corners throughout.
